# Coaching methods

Foundation and Daniels-inspired retain their existing behavior. Marathon endurance is an original, independent, mostly-easy template; it is not a branded schedule or individualized coaching service. Selecting a method edits the form only. Saved calendars change only after explicit save/regeneration; reviews adapt planned sessions using existing recovery safeguards.

## Marathon endurance

Phases use days until the next upcoming **main marathon**, including when another race intervenes: base beyond 84 days, build at 50–84 days, marathon-specific at 22–49 days, taper within 21 days. Every fourth plan week remains a cutback. Tune-up taper, race dates, race recovery and return-to-running reductions override the nominal phase. Without a remaining main marathon, training stays easy outside other race windows.

Weekly budgets retain the existing user growth and peak caps, cutbacks and taper factors. Conversational long runs are additionally limited to 35% of the weekly budget, 28 km, and the lower of the user's limit or 150 minutes, with growth anchored to the reported comfortable long run. Other runs stay easy. These limits can yield shorter long runs than familiar marathon schedules and do not establish readiness to race.

Marathon effort is optional and off by default. It requires confirmed history, current weekly distance ≥40 km, comfortable long run ≥14 km, ≥4 running days, and ≥4 elapsed plan weeks. Generated future mileage is not evidence of achieving these eligibility levels. Only non-cutback marathon-specific long runs at least 14 days from every race qualify. The segment is limited to 15% of the run, 5% of that week's budget and 15 minutes using the comfortable easy pace as a conservative distance estimate. Sub-kilometre work is omitted. The rest remains conversational. M means controlled sustainable marathon effort, with a return to easy if strained. No numerical marathon training pace is inferred; goal times never establish fitness. Displayed duration remains an easy-pace estimate.

All phase boundaries, eligibility checks, volume/time limits and recovery multipliers are independent product heuristics, not validated thresholds, injury predictions, or claims from the sources below. Recovery flags convert harder work to easy and remove segments when shortening the session. Illness/injury pauses training; missed work is not stacked, and a missed long-run focus can only move into an existing easy slot, capped by that slot, the skipped original/current long run, and the replacement date’s generated marathon long-run limits. Rescheduling removes harder segments; multiple-miss reductions also clear harder work even when volume was already reduced.

## Source context and limits

- [Hal Higdon, Novice 1](https://www.halhigdon.com/training-programs/marathon-training/novice-1-marathon/) discusses consistent preparation, easy long runs and rest. Those broad principles inform the easy-running emphasis; its schedule is not reproduced.
- [V.O2 marathon plan description](https://vdoto2.com/p/training-plan/marathon/1) describes coach review of training/racing history and individualized paces. This motivates distinguishing current capacity from aspirational goals; this product does not implement VDOT estimation or that coaching service.
- [Smyth and Lawlor (2021), Longer Disciplined Tapers Improve Marathon Performance for Recreational Runners](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2021.735220/full) reports observational associations in recreational runners. It supports considering tapering, not causal certainty or validation of this application's exact taper factors.
